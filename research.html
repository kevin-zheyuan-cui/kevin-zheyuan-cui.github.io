Term Structure with Investor Tax Heterogeneity: Application to the Muni Puzzle

Draft coming soon

I develop an equilibrium term-structure model in which investors differ in their tax treatment and jointly choose taxable and tax-exempt bonds across maturities under no-short-sale constraints. Top-bracket households receive the largest benefit from tax exemption and therefore anchor short-maturity tax-exempt municipal bond yields. Lower-tax investors value the exemption less, but those for whom duration is more valuable participate in municipals farther out the yield curve, where the relative benefit of bearing duration can offset their tax disadvantage. Endogenous participation therefore makes the tax rate implied by municipal-to-taxable yield ratios vary with maturity, rather than being set by a single marginal investor. This explains the municipal bond puzzle: the literature benchmarks municipal yields at every maturity against top-bracket households’ after-tax taxable yields. These closely match short municipal yields, but long municipal yields substantially exceed them. Without targeting municipal yields, the calibrated model accounts for 83 percent of the 117-basis-point gap at thirty years. Empirically, the pass-through of top-bracket household tax rates to municipal yields declines with maturity, while tax variation affecting lower-tax institutions is increasingly reflected in yields farther out the curve. These opposite maturity gradients support the mechanism and contradict the representative-tax benchmark, in which the top-bracket household’s tax rate applies at every maturity.

The Effects of Generative AI on High-Skilled Work: Evidence from Three Field Experiments with Software Developers

with Mert Demirer, Sonia Jaffe, Leon Musolff, Sida Peng and Tobias Salz

Management Science, forthcoming

Coverage: New York Times; 2025 Economic Report of the President

This study evaluates the effect of generative AI on software developer productivity via randomized controlled trials at Microsoft, Accenture, and an anonymous Fortune 100 company. These field experiments, run by the companies as part of their ordinary course of business, provided a random subset of developers with access to an AI-based coding assistant suggesting intelligent code completions. Though each experiment is noisy and results vary across experiments, when data is combined across three experiments and 4,867 developers, our analysis reveals a 26.08% increase (SE: 10.3%) in completed tasks among developers using the AI tool. Notably, less experienced developers had higher adoption rates and greater productivity gains.

Familiarity in International Equity Investment: Concentrated Value, Pervasive Allocation Bias

with Yinan Qiu

Does familiarity convey information or produce bias? Using 2000–2024 data from 1,663 active U.S. international equity funds and managers’ foreign education as a familiarity proxy, we examine both country allocation and stock selection. Funds overweight education-linked countries, yet these sub-portfolios do not outperform. Within familiar countries, the most active quartile of stocks earns a monthly five-factor alpha of 41 basis points, while the remaining holdings earn no significant alpha. The overweight persists after removing top-quartile stocks. Familiarity’s value is concentrated, its allocation footprint pervasive: information and bias coexist at margins that country-level averages obscure.

Relief without Resolution: Growth Targets and the Hidden Credit Risk of China’s Local Government Debt

with Tao Shen, Wei Xiong and Tianqi Zhi

Draft available upon request

We show that the debt of China’s local government financing vehicles (LGFVs) was built in different ways. Some provinces accumulated large debt stocks through dense networks of LGFVs built long ago; others borrowed to hit growth targets their economies could not deliver. With rationed access to the bond market, these overly ambitious provinces leaned on nonstandard debt: opaque, costly loans from trusts and leasing companies. They later suffered more defaults on nonstandard debt, slower growth and larger state rescues. The 2023 debt resolution program, which refinanced LGFV debt with government bonds, was meant to resolve this risk but largely transferred it. Bond prices stopped reflecting the fundamentals of LGFVs and their local governments; that information moved into nonstandard debt, whose spread over the same borrower’s bonds widened by more than 150 basis points. Relief made the bond market look safe while pushing the risk that growth targets created into the most opaque and costly corner of local government finance.

Public Debt Resolution and the Relocation of Local Information Rents: Evidence from Bank Wealth-Management Products in China

with Tao Shen and Tianqi Zhi

Draft available upon request

China’s 2023 program to defuse local government debt risk had an unintended cost for small local banks, which compete with national banks in the RMB 30 trillion wealth-management market through local credit. Their products hold three times the portfolio share of local government financing vehicle (LGFV) bonds that national banks’ products do, mostly from their home provinces, and these holdings deliver the higher returns and fewer losses that attract return-chasing investors. By moving LGFV risk onto provincial balance sheets, the program compressed credit spreads within and across provinces. The premium on home-province bonds fell by two-thirds, and local banks retreated. The premium largely survived in non-standard debt, negotiated claims with no public price, but only the largest local banks had the scale to move into it. De-risking public bonds relocated the return to local information into opaque claims that require scale, and the smallest banks lost most.
