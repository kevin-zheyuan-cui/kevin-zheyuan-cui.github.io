Job Market Paper

Tax Heterogeneity, Duration Risk, and the Term Structure: An Application to the Municipal Bond Puzzle

Paper SSRN

At short maturities, tax-exempt municipal yields closely match top-bracket households’ after-tax yields on comparable taxable bonds. At thirty years, municipal yields exceed this household benchmark by 118 basis points, implying a marginal tax rate below the top household rate. I explain this longstanding municipal bond puzzle with a term-structure model jointly pricing taxable and tax-exempt bonds. Heterogeneous taxes and balance sheets endogenously segment ownership across markets and maturities under short-sale constraints. High-tax households value the exemption most and hold shorter municipal bonds. Some lower-tax investors have long-duration liabilities that make rate risk cheaper to bear, so they hold long municipal bonds despite smaller exemption benefits. Because they also hold taxable bonds, relative long-term yields reflect their lower taxes. Without targeting municipal yields, the calibrated model explains 83 percent of the gap. Cross-state differences in home-state exemptions and a discontinuity in a bank-only tax benefit support the model’s predictions. Household taxes are almost fully reflected in short yields but less in long yields, while bank tax benefits mainly affect long yields.

Publication

The Effects of Generative AI on High-Skilled Work: Evidence from Three Field Experiments with Software Developers

with Mert Demirer, Sonia Jaffe, Leon Musolff, Sida Peng and Tobias Salz

Management Science, forthcoming

Coverage: New York Times; 2025 Economic Report of the President

Journal page SSRN

This study evaluates the effect of generative AI on software developer productivity via randomized controlled trials at Microsoft, Accenture, and an anonymous Fortune 100 company. These field experiments, run by the companies as part of their ordinary course of business, provided a random subset of developers with access to an AI-based coding assistant suggesting intelligent code completions. Though each experiment is noisy and results vary across experiments, when data is combined across three experiments and 4,867 developers, our analysis reveals a 26.08% increase (SE: 10.3%) in completed tasks among developers using the AI tool. Notably, less experienced developers had higher adoption rates and greater productivity gains.

Working Papers

Overvalued Geographic Familiarity in International Equity Management

with Yinan Qiu

Paper SSRN

Foreign education may provide country-specific expertise, but its value across international portfolios is less clear. Within funds over time and across similar funds, greater foreign-educated representation is associated with lower factor-adjusted net returns. High-conviction holdings in managers’ education countries earn high alphas, but contribute little to portfolio alpha at actual weights. Outside these countries, foreign-educated teams select stocks less successfully in markets bordering or more socially connected to their education countries, where familiarity is expected to help. We connect these investment patterns to how funds recruit and evaluate managers. Funds are more likely to hire managers educated in a country after their holdings there underperform. These appointments are followed by short-lived local improvements. More broadly, foreign-educated recruits bring weaker prior team investment records than U.S.-educated recruits, and foreign-educated managers’ retention is less sensitive to deteriorating fund performance than that of U.S.-educated colleagues. Together, the findings suggest that funds credit geographic familiarity more broadly than demonstrated investment performance warrants.

How Hidden Local Government Debt Shapes Shadow Banking Competition: Evidence from Wealth Management in China

with Tao Shen and Tianqi Zhi

Paper

Using granular holdings data on China’s RMB 33 trillion market for wealth-management products operated by banks or their subsidiaries, we study how government support for borrowers shapes competition in shadow banking. Smaller banks sought an edge over larger rivals by reaching for yield in home-province bonds issued by local government financing vehicles (LGFVs). The 2023 debt resolution initiative strengthened expected support for LGFVs and compressed their bond spreads. The estimated product-return premium associated with home LGFV bonds fell by 69%. Larger local banks expanded into higher-yielding, LGFV-linked private credit, while smaller banks substituted little and lost ground in relative product returns and wealth-management balances. Our model explains this unequal adjustment through fixed contracting costs that make investment scale important. Government support for borrowers can thus erode smaller banks’ competitive edge and shift shadow banking toward larger intermediaries and more opaque credit.

The Limits of Debt Discipline: Risk-Based Regulation and Shadow Finance

with Tao Shen, Wei Xiong and Tianqi Zhi

We study whether risk-based regulation makes public debt safer or simply shifts it to other regions and costlier lenders. China caps bond borrowing by local government financing vehicles (LGFVs) in high-risk regions, then refinanced their debt in 2023, mostly in those same regions. We find that this discipline largely moves the borrowing rather than removing it. First, it moves across regions. Borrowing slows in high-risk regions, which later grow more slowly, but keeps rising in regions seen as fiscally stronger despite low investment returns. Second, it moves into shadow finance. LGFVs facing rating-based bond limits turn to costly nonstandard loans from trusts and leasing companies. After the 2023 relief, their nonstandard rates rose 176 basis points relative to their bond rates. Relief aimed at the riskiest regions also invites moral hazard, as less-relieved regions keep expanding this costly nonstandard debt. Discipline can make the bond market look safe while risk builds where it is costlier and harder to see.
